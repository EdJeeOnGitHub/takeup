# Does Distance Curvature Identify \(\sigma_u\)?

This note reports three diagnostics for whether continuous-distance curvature
pins down the idiosyncratic decision-noise parameter \(\sigma_u\).

## 1. Curvature sensitivity

The take-up likelihood is decomposed into three local observation-weight
directions:

1. **Level:** the original take-up likelihood, with every observation weighted
   equally.
2. **Slope:** distance residualized against treatment-arm indicators.
3. **Curvature:** squared distance residualized against treatment-arm
   indicators and arm-specific linear distance slopes.

Slope and curvature weights are normalized to have observation-weighted RMS
one. Curvature is therefore orthogonal to both treatment-specific levels and
linear gradients. Because slope and curvature weights take both signs, these
are infinitesimal likelihood contrasts rather than independent likelihood
blocks.

For quantity \(h(\theta)\), the reported posterior-mean sensitivity is

\[
\frac{
\operatorname{Cov}_{post}
\left[h(\theta),\sum_c q_{rc}\ell_c(\theta)\right]
}{
\operatorname{sd}_{post}[h(\theta)]
},
\]

and posterior-uncertainty sensitivity is

\[
\frac{
\operatorname{Cov}_{post}
\left[
\{h(\theta)-E_{post}[h(\theta)]\}^2,
\sum_c q_{rc}\ell_c(\theta)
\right]
}{
2\operatorname{Var}_{post}[h(\theta)]
}.
\]

For \(\sigma_u\), the results are:

| Sensitivity | Level | Slope | Curvature |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Posterior mean | -0.03 | -0.28 | -0.06 |
| Log posterior SD | -0.02 | -0.31 | -0.33 |

Curvature reweighting barely changes the posterior mean of \(\sigma_u\), but
it contracts its posterior SD locally. This indicates that observations in
the curvature direction carry some information about \(\sigma_u\). It does
not establish that curvature uniquely identifies it because the contrast
does not allow the other structural parameters to be reoptimized separately.

Curvature is much more consequential for \(\lambda\), \(\delta\), social-image
returns, and several multipliers. In posterior-mean SD units:

| Quantity | Curvature sensitivity |
|:--|--:|
| \(\lambda\) | 1.20 |
| \(\delta\) | -2.02 |
| Social-image returns | 1.14 to 1.61 |
| Control multiplier | 2.47 to 2.55 |
| Bracelet multiplier | -0.27 to -0.45 |

## 2. Conditional Jacobian decomposition

For each of the 144 estimation clusters, predicted take-up is locally
regressed on the 16 standardized structural parameters over the 1,600
posterior draws. This gives a posterior-local model-moment Jacobian. The
\(\sigma_u\) derivative is projected on the Jacobian columns for the other 15
parameters using binomial-information weights. The remaining derivative is
then projected sequentially onto mutually orthogonal arm-specific level,
linear-slope, and quadratic-curvature subspaces.

The result is:

| Component | Conditional information | Share |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Level | \(4.96\times10^{-13}\) | 0.002% |
| Linear slope | \(2.62\times10^{-11}\) | 0.108% |
| Quadratic curvature | \(6.24\times10^{-10}\) | 2.57% |
| Higher-order/remainder | \(2.37\times10^{-8}\) | 97.32% |

Only \(2.19\times10^{-8}\) of the unconditional local
\(\sigma_u\)-derivative information remains after the other reported
parameters are allowed to adjust. Thus, in this posterior-local
linearization, the take-up profile generated by changing \(\sigma_u\) is
almost completely reproducible by changing other parameters. Quadratic
curvature accounts for only 2.6% of the very small residual.

This is evidence against claiming that simple distance curvature separately
identifies \(\sigma_u\). The Jacobian is estimated locally from posterior
variation, rather than by globally perturbing and resolving the model, so the
absolute information magnitude should be treated as a local diagnostic. Its
near-zero conditional rank is nevertheless consistent with strong parameter
substitution.

## 3. Close/Far-only ablation

Two new, matched four-chain models were estimated using the current code,
priors, data, and 400 retained iterations per chain:

- the continuous-distance structural model;
- a Close/Far-only model that replaces every cluster's exact standardized
  distance with its distance-group sample mean in both take-up and beliefs.

The retained group means correspond to approximately 0.671 km for Close and
1.808 km for Far. The marginal continuous-distance likelihood is disabled in
the ablation. All other structural restrictions and priors are unchanged.

The posterior comparison is:

| Specification | Mean | SD | 95% interval | \(\widehat R\) | Bulk ESS |
|:--|--:|--:|:--|--:|--:|
| Continuous distance | 0.404 | 0.222 | [0.140, 0.980] | 1.002 | 964 |
| Close/Far only | 0.410 | 0.218 | [0.144, 0.962] | 1.003 | 892 |

The Close/Far-to-continuous posterior-SD ratio is

\[
\frac{0.218}{0.222}=0.980.
\]

Both fits retained exactly 400 draws per chain, had zero divergences, and
passed CmdStan's treedepth, E-BFMI, effective-sample-size, and split-\(\widehat
R\) checks.

Removing within-group continuous-distance information therefore does not
widen the posterior for \(\sigma_u\). Its posterior location is also
essentially unchanged.

## Conclusion

The three diagnostics do not support the statement that observed
continuous-distance curvature pins down \(\sigma_u\):

- curvature-directed reweighting locally reduces uncertainty in \(\sigma_u\);
- after other structural parameters adjust, quadratic curvature contains only
  a small share of an already tiny unique \(\sigma_u\) derivative;
- eliminating within-Close/Far continuous-distance variation does not reduce
  posterior precision.

The defensible interpretation is that \(\sigma_u\) is disciplined mainly by
the model's joint equilibrium restrictions, normalization, parameter
tradeoffs, and potentially its prior, rather than being separately identified
by observed quadratic distance curvature. Curvature is empirically important
for \(\lambda\), \(\delta\), social-image returns, and social multipliers, but
that is a different claim from curvature uniquely identifying \(\sigma_u\).
