\subsection{A Reduced-Form Relative Distance Multiplier}
\label{app:reduced-form-distance-multiplier}

The structural model normalizes the observed distance response by a
counterfactual direct access-cost response with social image shut down. No
experimental arm has zero observability, so that absolute normalization
necessarily requires model structure. We can nevertheless recover the central
comparison across signal environments directly from the experiment. We pool
Control and Calendar as No Signal and Ink and Bracelet as Any Signal and
estimate the take-up response to distance in each group.

Table~\ref{tab:rf-distance-multiplier} reports three specifications. The first
uses the original randomized Close/Far assignment. The second estimates a
group-specific linear slope in realized community-centroid distance. The third
uses a natural cubic spline with three degrees of freedom and reports the average finite slope from
0.5 to 2.0 km, an interval inside the common empirical support of all four
arms. All specifications include arm indicators, county fixed effects, sex,
age, and the design-based expected-distance control. We use 1,000
county-stratified exponential community-weight draws for uncertainty.

The design-based comparison is especially clear. Moving from Close to Far
lowers take-up by 18.8 percentage points in No Signal communities and by 6.7
percentage points in Any Signal communities. The difference is 12.2
percentage points, with a 95 percent interval of 5.2 to 19.3 percentage
points. Public signals therefore leave 35 percent of the no-signal distance
response, corresponding to 65 percent attenuation. The continuous-distance
specifications are more conservative but yield the same ordering: the linear
estimate implies 39 percent attenuation and the flexible finite change implies
44 percent attenuation.

Figure~\ref{fig:rf-distance-curves} displays the flexible standardized take-up
curves. No Signal take-up falls persistently over the common support. The Any
Signal curve falls at short distances and then becomes flatter through the
middle of the experimental range. Because the experiment contains only 144
independent communities, a separate derivative at every point is much less
precise than the finite change. Figure~\ref{fig:rf-derivative-contrast} confirms
that both the pointwise and simultaneous 95 percent bands for the derivative
contrast include zero over the grid. We therefore do not claim that the two
derivatives differ at every distance. The stable reduced-form object is the
average finite-distance response reported in the table.

The derivatives are total equilibrium distance responses. Under the
restriction that the direct access-cost response is common across randomized
signal environments, their ratio identifies a relative multiplier:
\[
  \frac{\partial E[Y\mid d,\mathrm{Any\ Signal}]/\partial d}
       {\partial E[Y\mid d,\mathrm{No\ Signal}]/\partial d}
  =
  \frac{M_{\mathrm{Any\ Signal}}(d)}
       {M_{\mathrm{No\ Signal}}(d)}.
\]
This comparison requires neither latent types, fixed-point estimation, nor a
parametric reporting model. It does not identify either multiplier's absolute
level relative to zero observability. The reduced form therefore establishes
that public signals substantially flatten the distance response; the
structural model additionally supplies the absolute normalization and the
decomposition into visibility and motivation inference.
