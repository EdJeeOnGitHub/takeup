\subsection{Robustness to Within-Community Dependence}
\label{app:cluster-inference}

The baseline posterior conditions on the structural model's individual-level
sampling assumptions. We use a county-stratified cluster bootstrap as our
primary adjustment for dependence within communities. Each bootstrap draw
resamples whole communities with replacement within county and refits the same
structural objective; priors are not reweighted. The resulting percentile
intervals therefore preserve arbitrary within-community dependence while
respecting the study's geographic stratification.

Table~\ref{tab:cluster-bootstrap-ates} reports the bootstrap distribution of
the structural take-up effects. Bracelet remains positive, and its Far effect
and combined effect remain distinguishable from zero. Ink remains more
effective in Far than Close communities. The Any Signal--No Signal
Far-minus-Close contrast remains positive at its median, although its 95
percent interval just includes zero. As expected, the cluster-bootstrap
intervals are wider than the baseline posterior intervals.

For comparison, we also estimate a structural specification with an additive
community-level utility shock. This changes the behavioral model rather than
only its uncertainty calculation. The bootstrap and random-shock exercises
should therefore not be interpreted as interchangeable. The multiplier
comparison in Table~\ref{tab:cluster-multipliers} shows that the bootstrap
leaves point estimates close to baseline but widens uncertainty, while the
random-shock specification moves multipliers toward one.

Intervals for each treatment-specific multiplier answer whether a particular
curve differs from one at a particular distance. They are not, however, the
most direct uncertainty calculation for the paper's experimental comparison:
whether multiplier amplification is greater without a public signal than with
one. We therefore form this contrast within every bootstrap refit, preserving
the covariance among all four treatment-specific multiplier estimates. Define
No Signal as the average of Control and Calendar and Any Signal as the average
of Ink and Bracelet. Table~\ref{tab:cluster-multiplier-contrasts} reports the
paired difference. It is positive at all three prespecified distances and for
the finite 500--2500m change. At 1500m the estimate is 0.48, with a 95 percent
interval of $[0.06,1.34]$ and 99.8 percent of refits positive. As a conservative
whole-grid summary, the minimum of the contrast over 500m, 1500m, and 2500m is
0.42, with a 95 percent interval of $[0.04,1.11]$ and 99.3 percent of refits
positive. This is evidence for the pooled signal-versus-no-signal ordering; it
does not imply that every individual arm's multiplier is precisely different
from one.
