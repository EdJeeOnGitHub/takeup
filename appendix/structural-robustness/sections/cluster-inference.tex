\subsection{Robustness to Within-Community Dependence}
\label{app:cluster-inference}

The baseline posterior conditions on the structural model's individual-level
sampling assumptions. Our preferred cluster adjustment is a cluster
weighted-likelihood bootstrap. Within each county and refit, every community
first receives an independent unit-mean exponential draw; the draws are then
normalized to sum to the county's number of communities. The resulting
Dirichlet weights preserve the study's county stratification. Each weight
multiplies all of its community's contributions to the take-up, beliefs,
randomized-distance, and willingness-to-pay likelihoods. Priors are not
reweighted. Because our implementation weights the likelihood while leaving
the prior fixed, we describe it as a generalized Bayesian weighted-likelihood
bootstrap. Communities remain the indivisible weighting unit.

Unlike multinomial resampling, exponential weighting does not delete
communities from a refit. This preserves support in the randomized
treatment-by-distance cells and is especially useful for the nonlinear
fixed-point decomposition. It nevertheless perturbs the influence of whole
communities and therefore allows the fitted structural objects to reflect
cluster-level sampling variation. We report percentile intervals across 999
successful weighted refits; all 999 attempted refits completed and used
distinct weight vectors. Short posterior runs for prespecified audit cases confirmed
the mode approximation in 96.7 percent of audited cells.

Table~\ref{tab:cluster-bootstrap-ates} reports the weighted-refit distribution of
the structural take-up effects. Bracelet remains positive, and its Far effect
and combined effect remain distinguishable from zero. Ink remains more
effective in Far than Close communities. The Any Signal--No Signal
Far-minus-Close contrast is 0.050, with a 95 percent interval of
$(0.012,0.098)$. As expected, the weighted-likelihood intervals are wider than
the baseline posterior intervals.

For comparison, we also estimate a structural specification with an additive
community-level utility shock. This changes the behavioral model rather than
only its uncertainty calculation. The weighted-likelihood and random-shock exercises
should therefore not be interpreted as interchangeable. The multiplier
comparison in Table~\ref{tab:cluster-multipliers} shows that exponential
weighting leaves point estimates close to baseline but widens uncertainty, while the
random-shock specification moves multipliers toward one.

Intervals for each treatment-specific multiplier answer whether a particular
curve differs from one at a particular distance. They are not, however, the
most direct uncertainty calculation for the paper's experimental comparison:
whether multiplier amplification is greater without a public signal than with
one. We therefore form this contrast within every weighted refit, preserving
the covariance among all four treatment-specific multiplier estimates. Define
No Signal as the average of Control and Calendar and Any Signal as the average
of Ink and Bracelet. Table~\ref{tab:cluster-multiplier-contrasts} reports the
paired difference. It is positive in every weighted refit at each of the three
prespecified distances. At 1500m the estimate is 0.47, with a 95 percent
interval of $[0.12,1.18]$; the finite-change analogue over 500--2500m is also
0.47, with interval $[0.12,1.18]$. As a conservative three-point summary, the minimum
of the contrast over 500m, 1500m, and 2500m is 0.42, with a 95 percent interval
of $[0.10,1.01]$. This is evidence for the pooled signal-versus-no-signal ordering; it
does not imply that every individual arm's multiplier is precisely different
from one.
