\subsection{Sensitivity to Signal-Specific Social-Image Weights}
\label{app:signal-specific-lambda}

The baseline model restricts the social-image weight $\lambda$ to be common
across experimental arms. We assess this restriction using nested
specifications with a common weight, two weights pooling Ink and Bracelet as
Any Signal and Control and Calendar as No Signal, and four arm-specific
weights. The flexible specifications parameterize log-weight contrasts around
a common geometric mean. This keeps the common level of social-image concerns
separate from treatment-arm heterogeneity in those concerns.

Tables~\ref{tab:lambda-nested-ates} and
\ref{tab:lambda-nested-multipliers} show that allowing signal-specific weights
has little effect on the model-implied treatment effects or the qualitative
multiplier pattern. In all three specifications, the multiplier is above one
for Control and Calendar and below one for Ink and Bracelet. The flexible
models modestly move all multipliers toward one.

Because extra weights can be weakly identified, we vary the prior standard
deviation of pairwise log-weight ratios. Table~\ref{tab:lambda-prior} reports
the resulting contrast and multiplier estimates at 1.5 km. The posterior
contrasts remain near zero, and the multiplier conclusions are stable across
the prior grid. The contraction statistic is reported to distinguish a stable
posterior caused by data from one inherited mechanically from the prior.

Finally, a simulation exercise fits the common, grouped, and arm-specific
models to data generated under common, grouped, arm-heterogeneous, and
no-image designs. Table~\ref{tab:lambda-recovery} reports recovery of the image
weight and multiplier. The grouped and arm-specific models recover genuine
heterogeneity substantially better than the common-weight restriction, while
the more flexible specifications do not generate large multiplier errors when
the true weight is common.
