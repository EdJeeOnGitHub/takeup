\subsection{Robustness of the Policy Exercise across Structural Models}
\label{app:policy-model-robustness}

We repeat the optimal-allocation exercise under the alternative structural
models considered above. Each exercise uses the same 3.5 km feasible-site set,
the same welfare target---predicted take-up under the experimental
allocation---and the same information counterfactuals. The specifications vary
the observability measure, permit signal-specific social-image weights, replace
Gaussian intrinsic motivation with a variance-normalized Student-$t(5)$
distribution, or add a community random shock. We omit rather than relabel the
older multinomial cluster-resampling policy output, so this table focuses on
the alternative structural-model posteriors.

Table~\ref{tab:policy-model-robustness-contrasts} reports the principal policy
contrast. Giving the policymaker the Bracelet rather than Control observability
profile produces a positive and economically large median saving under correct
classification, perceived second-order observability, the
Any-Signal/No-Signal social-image specification, Student-$t$ types, and the
community random-shock model. The fully treatment-specific social-image model
has a similar median but a much wider interval, consistent with the weaker
identification created by allowing a separate social-image weight for every
treatment.

When social-image returns are instead held fixed at their 0.5 km level, the
median saving falls to 1--11 PoTs and most intervals include zero. The gap
between the two columns indicates that the larger policy savings arise through
the endogenous interaction between distance and social image, rather than
through a mechanical level difference between observability regimes.

The calculations use every retained HMC draw: 800 draws for
each observability fit, 1,600 for each signal-specific-lambda and Student-$t$
fit, and 4,000 for the community-shock fit. Thus, the displayed intervals
summarize posterior uncertainty. For a draw whose welfare target cannot
be attained, we retain the best feasible allocation and flag the draw as
target-infeasible. If a counterfactual has no fixed point, we retain the draw in
the diagnostic denominator, mark its equilibrium as undefined, and exclude it
from that scenario's reported quantiles rather than insert a pseudo-root. The
full results and these diagnostics appear in
Table~\ref{tab:policy-model-robustness}.
