\section{The reduced-form take-up interaction is less decisive than the framing suggests}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Problem.} The headline interaction rests on realized Close/Far: 6.8pp,
    $p=0.083$. The original randomized assignment gives a stronger result, so the choice
    of principal specification is consequential --- the editor wants the randomization
    back at the center.
  \item \textbf{What we worked on.} Swap the principal take-up specification to original
    assignment. Table~\ref{tab:memo-itt-pair} shows the swap in the paper's own table
    format: same specification, Any Signal $=$ No Signal (Far--Close) moves from
    $p=0.083$ to $p=0.001$. The relative-multiplier analysis
    (Table~\ref{tab:memo-rf-multiplier}, Figure~\ref{fig:memo-rf-curves}) turns this
    into the paper's central object: signals remove 65\% of the No-Signal distance
    response (39--44\% continuous).
  \item \textbf{Takeaway.} Reorder Section 4 so original assignment is primary and
    realized/continuous are sensitivity; flag PAP status at first use. Caution: the
    Bracelet--Calendar take-up interaction is \emph{not} decisive even under original
    assignment ($p=0.145$--$0.237$), so take-up claims stay pooled.
\end{itemize}

\begin{decision}
  We will swap to original assignment -- need to re-run a lot of specs. Also add randomization inference p-values if we can.
  \newline
  TBD: If we should include attenuation results for binary, linear distance, and spline -- and whether these should be just signal/no signal or 
  also bra-cal.
\end{decision}

\section{The observability measure does not match the theoretical object}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Problem.} Definite-answer share is not the probability of being
    observed; bracelets and ink are one-sided noisy signals. A referee will demand an
    asymmetric observation model.
  \item \textbf{What we do.} Full model re-estimated under correct classification and
    perceived second-order observability: stable (Table~\ref{tab:memo-obs-alt}). Explicit
    asymmetric-reporting model --- truth-specific Yes/No/Don't-know matrix, false
    positives and negatives separate, four regularizations
    (Tables~\ref{tab:memo-asym}--\ref{tab:memo-asym-contrast}).
  \item \textbf{Takeaway.} ATEs stable (Bracelet $+7.2$pp). The pooled No Signal $-$ Any
    Signal contrast survives every reporting model (0.47 at 1500m; 0.45 finite). The
    Bracelet-specific multiplier does not (finite 0.34, interval $(-0.04,0.82)$), and the
    500m contrast is $\approx 0$. The robust object is the pooled contrast, not
    arm-specific levels.
\end{itemize}

\begin{decision}
  Do not include asymmetric model for now, report robustness in SM across all other observability models. Add Pr(amplification), Pr(mitigation) to 
  all SM tables.
\end{decision}

\subsection{Uncertainty too optimistic / pseudo-replication}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Issue.} Individual-level likelihood, 144-cluster design; structural
    intervals implausibly tighter than the reduced form.

    weights, one weight per community across all likelihood blocks

\subsection{Information structure}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Issue.} Full information eliminates Control amplification; dismissing it
    on fit alone is not sufficient. The motivation-inference channel ($\Delta'$) remains
    model-implied.
  \item \textbf{What we did and archived.} We coded the only free-text block linking
    inference content to randomized distance (endline item-meaning responses; two blind
    LLM coders, 99.4\% agreement). Result: a denotational null --- 95--98\% bare
    participation markers, 3 of 831 strings with any motivation content, no distance
    gradient. Because the question elicits what the item \emph{means}, not what it says
    about the wearer, this does not discipline $\Delta'$ either way; we decided not to
    include it. Archived in \path{ref-reports/signal-meaning-coding/} for a ``did you
    look?'' referee reply. One coding-free by-product is available if wanted: reported
    item exposure falls with randomized distance (bracelet $-3.7$pp, ink $-12.3$pp,
    calendar $-22.9$pp), a model-free observability fact.
  \item \textbf{Takeaway.} Concede Control-arm \emph{amplification} is
    information-structure dependent; the mitigation ordering is not. $\Delta'$ is
    presented as a model-implied decomposition.
\end{itemize}

\begin{decision}
  I think we leave this as is.
\end{decision}

\section{The policy exercise outruns the evidence}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Problem.} Extrapolates to 3.5km, ignores costs and weights, assumes
    observability transports under consolidation; 108-vs-81 too prominent in the
    abstract.
  \item \textbf{What we do.} Population weighting: saving 25 sites $[16,31]$ (24
    $[7,31]$ cluster-weighted; positive in every draw,
    Table~\ref{tab:memo-policy-pop}). Observability dilution under pooling: saving 24
    $\to$ 10 $[3,20]$ at 50\%, $\to$ 3 $[0,9]$ at 100\%
    (Table~\ref{tab:memo-policy-pooling}). Costs priced, including the \$0.20 bracelet:
    break-even site cost \$261 (travel unpriced) to \$483 (at \$0.10/km)
    (Table~\ref{tab:memo-policy-breakeven}, Figure~\ref{fig:memo-policy-breakeven}). A
    2.5km cap is shown mechanically infeasible (site floor 114 regardless of demand,
    Table~\ref{tab:memo-policy-cap}). Contrast positive across all structural models
    (Table~\ref{tab:memo-policy-models}).
  \item \textbf{Takeaway.} The defensible claim is conditional: signals save sites in
    every draw; the magnitude is 3--25 depending on observability transport, and
    resource savings depend on site and travel costs. Before locking numbers: reconcile
    main-text 108-vs-81 (fit105) with the corrected all-1{,}451-site geography
    (101-vs-76/77) --- the old 1{,}092-site object was a row-index bug --- and fix the
    80-vs-81 inconsistency.
\end{itemize}

\begin{decision}
  Don't worry about observability transport. Update w/ population weighting as main version? Add break even part. Add discussion on georgraphic 
  feasibility? Add robustness to alternative models.
\end{decision}
