## Sample Size

The key question here is how the monitored sample was generated: Specifically, how do we end up with 9.8k individuals even though our PAP implies fewer? **– in yellow highlighted below write-up of what we think might have happened**

Our understanding of the sample size selection:

1. We did a census and listed 39k individuals.  
2. We drew a random baseline sample of 15 (+2 backup households). We were ‘meant’ to have one individual per HH, in reality we have more than one person per HH.  
3. Monitoring at PoTs:  
   1. It should be: SMS control (17 phone owners \+ 8 non-phone owners) \+ SMS Treatment. However, in practice we sampled more phone owners and non-phone owners–within and across households–sometimes using different numbers for wave 1 vs. wave 2\.   
   2. However, we have also thrown in the baseline sample **which comprises 2,650 individuals, \~1/5th the sample, but is not preregistered. What is our justification for this this?**  
   3. How exactly is monitored calculated? In the code, we find: 

   *The "monitored" sample is defined differently by wave: wave 2 uses SMS treatment*         

     *assignment and baseline sample membership, while wave 1 relies on a lookup in*            

     *takeup\_survey\_dict\_wave1.csv. This wave 1 dictionary file (5,725 individuals) maps survey person\_key to census KEY.individ, but the code that created it is not in the repository. The file appears to be a pre-computed merge created externally or by code that was later removed.* 

   

All this to say, do we have any way of telling how we drew the monitored individuals \- and more to the point, can we justify why the baseline sample is included as monitored individuals given their omission in the PAP. Do we have any way of telling how we drew the SMS control individuals and therefore why we end up with a much larger SMS control sample?

## Specific concerns 

* **PAP–implementation mismatch (documentation gap):** Several sampling steps described in the PAP (especially SMS sampling and baseline sampling) do not match what was implemented in code (e.g., 15 HH/1 adult vs 17 HH/up to 2 adults; individual-based SMS counts vs household-based selection with within-HH phone-owner rules).  
* **Differential reach/consent in SMS treatment recruitment may create imbalance:** The SMS treatment sample is conditioned on being reached and consenting; individuals not reached or not consenting were not monitored as treated, raising the risk of **imbalance between treatment and control** that reflects recruitment success rather than pure random assignment.  
* **Wave 1 monitoring omission (non-phone owners):** **989** wave 1 non-phone owners were accidentally not included in monitoring due to a SurveyCTO coding/list-generation error, meaning the intended SMS-control monitoring roster (8,144) does not match the monitored reality (7,155).  
* **Knowledge module missingness/imbalance risk:** Field notes suggest that not everyone received the endline knowledge module, and **255** knowledge-table observations are missing; this could induce **arm-specific missingness** if the issue was not random (e.g., correlated with backups/reconsent lists or enumerator practices).  
* **Endline treatment sample size deviates from PAP “main target”:** SMS-treatment endline completions (**1,043**) exceed the PAP planned **880**, largely because backups were surveyed; this needs to be clearly tied to the intended estimand (e.g., define whether analysis is “main roster only” vs “main \+ backups”).

### Sample Details – backward engineered from data, code and PAP

**Household census.** In the pre-analysis plan (PAP), we stated that we would conduct a full household census in each cluster—enumerating all households and all eligible adults (18 years or older)—across **150 clusters**. In practice, the sampling code and downstream study components relied on the census roster as the master frame, but the final field implementation covered **144 clusters**. In the field, enumerators completed the census in **150** (144) clusters and recorded **42,441** (39,301) adults, that is including the six clusters that would later be dropped. **Baseline survey.** The PAP specified that we would randomly sample **15 households per cluster** and interview **one adult per household**, targeting **N \= 2,160** individuals. In the implemented sampling code, we instead randomly sampled **17 households per cluster** and allowed sampling of **up to 2 individuals per household** across **144 clusters**, generating a baseline roster of **2,414 households** and **4,185 individuals** (slightly below the implied 2,448 households, because in some clusters we only selected **15 or 12 households**). In the field, enumerators attempted to reach 2,160 individuals and successfully reached **2,062** **2,069** individuals. Among those reached, **38** were unable to participate (death/illness/language barrier) and **29** did not consent, yielding **1,995 completed baseline surveys**, which constitutes the baseline survey analysis sample. The full **4,185 person** baseline roster was added to the sample frame of individuals whose deworming take-up was monitored at points of treatment. 

**SMS treatment assignment and recruitment.** The PAP described SMS treatment sampling as follows: in control clusters, assign **15 individuals** to Social Information SMS and **15 individuals** to Reminder Only SMS, and in incentive clusters (bracelet/ink/calendar) assign **25 individuals** to Social Information SMS, corresponding to a planned total of **N\_reminder \= 510** and **N\_social info \= 3,260** (total **3,770**). In the implemented sampling code, we sampled **17 individuals** for Social Information and **17 individuals** for Reminder Only in control clusters, and **29 individuals** for Social Information in incentive clusters—then sampling **up to two phone owners within the household** of a sampled individual. In the field, enumerators reached **3,040 individuals**, of whom **18** did not consent. The final recruited SMS treatment sample was **N \= 3,022**, split into **387** Reminder Only and **2,635** Social Info recipients (with non-consent recorded as **2** in control social info recruitment and **16** in incentive social info recruitment). Only recruited and successfully consented individuals were added to the sample frame of individuals whose **deworming take-up was monitored as points of treatment.** 

**SMS control assignment for phone owners.** The PAP specified that, among phone owners, we would randomly sample **17 adults per cluster** (target **N\_first \= 2,448**, with the caveat that some clusters might contain fewer eligible phone owners). In the implemented sampling code, we drew **17 phone owners per cluster** (yielding **N \= 2,441**) and added a backup draw of **12 additional phone owners per cluster in wave 1** (68 cluster, N \= 765\) and **8 additional phone owners per cluster in wave 2** (76 cluster, N \= 607)—leading to a total of **N \= 3,813** phone owners. This 3,813 person phone owner sample constitutes the SMS control group used for comparison with the SMS treatment group to estimate the average treatment effect of Social Information and Reminder Only SMS on deworming take-up. Where possible we sampled an additional phone owner within the same household (**N \= 1,990)**, producing a combined phone owner SMS control roster of **N \= 5,803 monitored for deworming take-up at points of treatment.** 

**SMS control assignment for non-phone owners.** The PAP specified that among non-phone owners we would randomly sample **8 adults per cluster** (target **N \= 1,152**, with the caveat that some clusters might contain fewer eligible non phone owners). In the implemented sampling code, we drew **8 non-phone owners per cluster** (yielding **N \= 1,138**) and added a backup draw of **4 additional non-phone owners per cluster** (N \= 576). Where possible we sampled an additional non-phone owner within the same household (N \= 628), producing a combined non-phone owner SMS control roster of **N \= 2,341.** In field monitoring implementation, the non-phone owner roster was intended to be monitored alongside the phone owner SMS control roster, but **989 wave 1 non-phone owners** were accidentally not included in monitoring due to a SurveyCTO coding/list-generation error, reducing the SMS control monitored sample to **N \= 1,352** relative to the intended roster.

**Monitored sample (PoT monitoring lists).** The PAP conceptualized monitoring around the experimental SMS arms and SMS-control comparisons, with the census as the underlying frame. In implementation, monitoring lists were built from three independently drawn subsamples from the census roster: (i) the recruited SMS treatment group (**N \= 3,022**), (ii) the SMS control rosters of phone owners and non-phone owners (**N \= 7,155** \= 5,803 \+ 1,352), and (iii) the baseline selected roster (**N \= 4,185**). Because assignment/sampling for these subsamples was conducted independently using the full census, there is overlap across subsamples (i.e., some individuals were selected into baseline and also into SMS treatment or SMS control). After accounting for overlaps the total monitored sample is **N \= 12,827** adults and the main take-up analysis sample excluding the SMS treatment arm is **N \= 9,805** (= 12,827 − 3,022).

**Endline survey.** The PAP planned an endline survey with sampling by arm within clusters—Control clusters: **5 Reminder Only**, **5 Social Information**, and **15 SMS control**; Incentive clusters: **5 Social Information** and **20 SMS control**—implying planned totals of **880 SMS treatment** respondents and **2,710 SMS control** respondents. In the implemented endline sampling code, we drew endline respondents from the combined SMS treatment and SMS control rosters (total frame reported as **N \= 11,166**) and selected **2,706** SMS control respondents plus backups (reported as **284**, approximately 2 per cluster) and **880** SMS treatment respondents plus backups (reported as **350**, approximately 2 per cluster), yielding **N \= 3,586** in the main endline roster and **N \= 4,21820** including backups. In the field, enumerators reached **2,774** **2,745** SMS control respondents and completed **2,686** **2,673 NEW: 2,662** SMS control endline surveys (with **72** unable to participate and **16** refusals), and reached **1,056** **1,031** SMS treatment respondents and completed **1,043** **1,021 NEW: 1,024** SMS treatment endline surveys (with **6** unable and **7** refusals) (total unable: 78, total refusal: 23). The SMS treatment completion count exceeds the PAP “main” target of 880\. Finally, the endline included two different knowledge modules (“Knowledge Table A/B”) that respondents were randomly assigned to. However, **255 respondents** have missing knowledge table data, concentrated among backup and reconsent/replacement cases. N.B. data fabricated by enumerator 111 so cluster.id 1163 removed.
